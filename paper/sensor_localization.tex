
\section{Automated Sensor Localization}
To bridge the gap between the sparse temperature readings and the dense visual data, we developed an automated method to localize the four temperature sensors within the video frames. The sensors appear as bright, circular regions of high intensity. However, simply thresholding often yields false positives due to reflections or noise. We therefore enforce a geometric constraint based on the known physical layout of the sensors.

\subsection{Methodology}
Our robust localization pipeline consists of the following steps:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic ROI Cropping (Active Zone):}
    Before detection, we identify the relevant "Active Zone" to eliminate high-intensity artifacts outside the region of interest. We compute the vertical projection of the frame intensity (row-wise mean) to find the peak row $y_{peak}$. The image is then cropped to the region $[y_{peak} - 180, y_{peak} + 180]$. This dynamic cropping accounts for vertical shifts in the setup across different experiments while preserving the sensors.

    \item \textbf{Preprocessing and Enhancement:} 
    We average the first 15 frames of the video sequence to suppress transient noise. To assist detection in varying lighting conditions, we apply Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) to the cropped grayscale image, followed by Gaussian blurring.

    \item \textbf{Candidate Detection:} 
    We apply a binary threshold to the enhanced image and extract contours. Candidates are filtered based on geometric properties typical of the sensors:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Area constraint: $20 < \text{Area} < 5000$ pixels.
        \item Radius constraint: $r < 60$ pixels.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Pattern Optimization (Axis-Aligned):} 
    Let $C = \{c_1, c_2, ..., c_N\}$ be the set of valid candidate centroids. We seek the subset $S \subset C$ of size 4 that best approximates an \textit{axis-aligned} rectangle. 
    We iterate through all combinations of 4 candidates and minimize a cost function that penalizes:
    \begin{equation}
        Cost = \Delta_{width} + \Delta_{height} + \Delta_{diag} + 2 \cdot \text{AlignmentPenalty}
    \end{equation}
    where $\Delta$ represents the normalized difference between opposite sides and diagonals, and $\text{AlignmentPenalty}$ penalizes deviations from horizontal and vertical axes. This strictly enforces the grid layout, rejecting rotated parallelograms.
    
    \item \textbf{Grid Assignment:} 
    The four selected points are mapped back to the original full-frame coordinates and sorted to assign identifiers M1--M4.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Verification}
This combinatorial optimization approach proved significantly more robust than simple K-Means clustering or naive sorting, achieving 100\% detection rate across the dataset (15/15 videos) compared to unstable results with simpler methods.
